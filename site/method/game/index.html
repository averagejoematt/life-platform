The Game, Explained

The character sheet is a game — and a game you can't audit is just a badge. This page is the rulebook: every number below is generated from the exact config and constants the engine runs (engine v1.10.0 · config v1.7.0), never hand-typed, and a drift check fails the build if the rules change without this page regenerating.

The seven pillars

Every night the engine scores the instrumented pillars, each 0–100, from weighted components. Each component is a habit, another behavior (training, logging food, reading) or a sensor (something a device measures). Only the daily habits are two-state: an unlogged habit is a habit that didn't happen, so it scores 0 at full weight. Any other component with no data is unknown — the engine has no evidence either way, so it drops out of the weight sum and lowers the day's coverage instead (ADR-104, narrowed in 2026-11). Missing information is never read as decline. The instrumented pillars' weights below sum to 93%; the headline renormalizes over them.

A handful of component targets — sleep duration, deep and REM share — are not fixed opinions: once at least 30 observations of Matthew's own history exist, the target is re-derived monthly as the 75th percentile of his own distribution, clamped inside documented safety bounds (ADR-105 rule 4: thresholds come from personal variance, not authored taste). Until a metric clears that floor, the numbers below stand — explicitly labeled a population prior. Every derived target carries its provenance: method, window, and sample size.

Daily steps is the exception, because it is a commitment rather than a habit to measure: its target is the training plan's every-day floor and it scores full marks at the plan's step target, the same two numbers the morning coaching line states. Deriving it from his own history would let a run of low days lower the bar.

A day becomes XP

On a day the engine could actually judge, each pillar's raw score lands in a fixed band. A good day earns XP; a decent-but-unremarkable day earns nothing (ADR-134's zero point); a low day also earns nothing. XP never goes down: there is no daily decay, no negative balance and no debt — missing data, inactivity and rest cost no XP at all, and a day with too little evidence carries no XP judgment in either direction.

A level is 100 XP deep. The band's signed value — the shield column — moves a small shield (capped at 40) that acts as demotion insurance: good days fill it, measured low days drain it, and a day without evidence leaves it alone. A level-down can only land once the shield is under 20. It is flip-flop insurance against noisy data; silence never drains it.

How a level actually moves

Raw scores are smoothed by an exponential moving average (default λ 0.85 over a 21-day window; each pillar can override λ, see the pillar cards). The smoothed score, rounded, is the target level — but no level ever jumps to its target. Four gates stand in the way:

  • The streak gate. The target must hold above the current level for a full streak of days to climb (longer to fall — see the tier ladder). One great or terrible day moves nothing.
  • The lived-day gate. A climb also requires today's own measured performance to be at the target — the pre-blend, pre-boost number. EMA momentum after the behavior stopped can't buy a level, and in total logging silence the measured day is 0, so no dark day ever supports a climb (ADR-104).
  • The coverage gate. Below 50% data coverage the day carries no leveling signal at all: streaks hold, levels freeze in both directions. Thin data is smoothed toward neutral for display, and neutral must never be climbable — nor should a pillar crash on no information.
  • The shield. A level-down additionally waits until the shield is depleted (see above) — which only measured low days can do.

When a gate finally opens, the step size scales with the honest gap, so a pillar far from its target converges instead of marching one level at a time:

gap between target and current levelstep per move
more than 25 levels3 levels
more than 10 levels2 levels
anything smaller1 level

The tier ladder

The 100 levels group into five tiers, and the streak gates get progressively harder as the tiers rise — holding Elite demands more proof than leaving Foundation. Falling always takes a longer streak than climbing (a slump has to prove itself harder than a surge), and crossing a tier boundary in either direction demands the longest streaks of all.

Cross-pillar effects

Pillars aren't independent — the engine models a small set of spillovers as multiplicative modifiers on the day's scores. Every active effect applies (they stack), and each is announced on the character sheet when live. Boosts raise the level a pillar converges to, but never the lived-day bar — a standing boost can't demand a day nobody can perform (see the gates above).

These effects started life as authored priors — plausible physiology, written by hand, not measured. Every quarter the engine re-fits each one against the lived data as a lagged driver→next-day-target relationship (moving-block bootstrap 95% CI, false-discovery-rate corrected, autocorrelation-honest n). An effect earns the badge fitted only when its interval excludes no-effect in the claimed direction — and can lose it again at the next fit. The live badge (with its n and interval) is on the character sheet; a prior that fails to confirm is published on the wrong page as a finding (#1411, ADR-105).

When the data goes dark

The game's hardest honesty rules are about absence (ADR-104, amended 2026-11). The sheet can never coast on silence — and it can never read silence, inactivity or rest as decline.

  • Unlogged habits score zero; everything else missing is unknown. A daily habit not logged is a habit that didn't happen. Any other missing input — a quiet wearable, an unlogged meal, no reading session, no workout log — drops out of the weight sum instead of scoring zero.
  • Missing qualitative information is unknown, not decline. How often the journal is written is not scored, and nothing a language model reads into the journal is scored: a day without an entry moves no score.
  • A day with no evidence moves nothing. When a pillar has no data, or too little to judge, that day is held: it is left out of the smoothed score, the cross-pillar variance, the all-pillars streak and the mood, and it earns or costs no XP. Thin days still show a value blended toward neutral, for display only.
  • No neglect decay. A quiet stretch — a week off logging — does not shrink any score and does not push a level down. A level falls only when measured days, sustained over the streak gate, say it should.
  • Movement counts what was recorded. The published Movement score counts sessions, strength days, Zone 2 minutes and kinds of movement over its weekly windows as recorded, with no adjustment for rest.
  • A rest day is not missing data. The day's data completeness expects a Strava row only when another source (Hevy, Whoop, the watch) shows a session that day.
  • Old lab results fade as evidence, not as health. As a blood panel ages its weight in Metabolic tapers — full for 30 days, half at 90, none at 180 — and the aged share is unknown; the markers' score itself never drops with age.
  • An uninstrumented pillar is out, not a placeholder. Relationships has no defensible, approved assessment yet, so it is not scored: no number stands in for it anywhere — not in the headline, the consistency variance, the effects or the mood.

The headline number — and the mood

The headline Character Level is the weight-averaged mean of the pillar levels, renormalized over the pillars that are instrumented and have earned a level, then floored — never rounded up. It understates by construction.

The character's mood — unavailable · fading · steady · thriving — is equally deterministic (ADR-105): pure code over the presence signal and the 7-day composite raw-score trend of the measured days. Fading needs the measured days themselves to fall (a trend of −5 or worse); when logging has simply gone quiet the mood is unavailable — not observed, never read as decline; thriving demands active logging, a trend of +3 or better, and a composite of at least 55; everything else is steady. No model ever decides how the character feels.

What's deterministic, what's judged

Deterministic: everything on this page. Scores, XP, levels, tiers, effects and mood are pure code over logged data — the same inputs always produce the same character, and no language model sits anywhere in the loop (ADR-104/105).

Judged: only the words around the numbers. The coaches' commentary, the weekly chronicle, and the narrative surfaces are AI-written about these pre-computed numbers, grounded by the platform's generation gates — the narration can characterize a level, but it can never move one.

And a boundary worth stating: the game is a motivational lens on real data, not a medical score. Every input is correlative and N=1.
